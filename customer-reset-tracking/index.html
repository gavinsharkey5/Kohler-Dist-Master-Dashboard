Does a Reset Lift Sales?

Every shelf/cooler reset (the "SFS" program), compared against the same calendar window one year earlier at that same account — controls for seasonality the same way the prior program years' own methodology did. Two cohorts, evaluated separately: 2026 resets (Jan–May) and 2025 resets (Jan–June). First-time resets (brand-new to the program) are tracked separately from repeat resets, since combining them hides very different effects.

Data quality note: both source exports carry a "Misc" Brand Family bucket — opaque, not tied to any specific brand, and wildly lumpy (tens of thousands of cases at one account in a single month, then nothing for months) — making up roughly 30% of raw case volume in both files. It's excluded from every number on this page (see generate.py); before that exclusion, an early build of this page showed swings well into the hundreds of percent driven almost entirely by which accounts happened to have a "Misc" spike land inside their reset window, not anything reset-related. With it excluded, these numbers land close to an earlier, independently-built version of this dashboard that used a completely different (daily, Case Equivalent) data source — a good sign, not a coincidence to ignore.

    Methodology & data notes

    3-month window: the reset's own calendar month plus the following two months (e.g. a March reset pairs March+April+May), compared against that same 3-month window one year earlier at the same account. Sales inputs here are monthly account totals, not a dated transaction ledger, so windows are whole calendar months, not a rolling 90-day span from the exact reset date.

    YTD window: January through the latest fully-elapsed month in that cohort's data (), vs. the same January–that-month range one year earlier.

    Lift %: (post total − pre total) / pre total, on Cases — the only metric in these exports (no $ Volume or Gross Profit here, unlike an earlier build of this dashboard).
